How it works

Four ways in. Any one of them is enough. Every action asks you to confirm.

👁️

Gaze control

A compatible webcam estimates where you are looking and picks one of nine large screen zones.

How: sit facing the camera, look at a zone.

🎙️

Voice control

Say a supported command such as "Hey Cursor, open Chrome". English works now; Hindi and Tamil are planned where available.

How: say "Hey Cursor", then the command.

🔦

Scanning mode

The computer highlights options one at a time, so you can choose without any precise movement.

How: wait for the highlight, then select.

⌨️

Single blink or key

One deliberate blink or any single key press selects the highlighted item, where technically supported.

How: blink once, or press Space.

Gaze and voice check each other

  • They agree: it goes ahead, after your confirmation.
  • They disagree: it asks "Did you mean A or B?" It never guesses.
  • One is missing: the other one carries it.

    Designed around your abilities, not your limitations.

    The problem

    Most computers assume a hand on a mouse or a clear voice. People with motor or speech differences can be locked out, even when their minds are fully able.

    Who it is for

    People who cannot reliably use their hands or speak clearly, including people with cerebral palsy, ALS, high spinal cord injury, locked-in syndrome, muscular dystrophy, severe arthritis, or temporary limits. Not everyone with the same diagnosis has the same abilities, so every method works alone or together.

    Why affordable matters

    Assistive technology should not be a luxury. Able Cursor aims to use the webcam and microphone a person already has. It is a prototype, and it will not work for every person or every disability.

    Indian Standards (BIS) and accessibility

    Able Cursor is designed with reference to IS 17802 (Part 1):2021, Accessibility for the ICT Products and Services, Part 1: Requirements. This is the Bureau of Indian Standards' technical adoption of the European standard EN 301 549 v3.2.1. The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Rules, as amended in 2023, name IS 17802 (Parts 1 and 2) as the standards for websites and applications. Part 2 (IS 17802 (Part 2):2022) describes how to determine conformance, and we used its approach for a self-check.

    Honest status: this is a self-assessment by students. It has not been audited or certified, and we do not claim full conformance.

    Limited manipulation or strength

    The core aim. Gaze, dwell, double blink, scanning, a single key and voice all work without a mouse or fine hand control. Mouse and keyboard still work for helpers.

    Limited cognition or learning

    Step-by-step onboarding, plain-language mode, consistent control positions, large buttons and a confirm step before risky actions.

    Vision and colour

    Three text sizes, high-contrast mode, visible focus outlines, no reliance on colour alone, and live status text for screen readers.

    Motion and seizures

    A reduce-motion setting turns animations off. Animations here are slow and nothing is designed to flash.

    Privacy

    Camera frames are processed on the device and never uploaded. Camera and microphone start only after explicit consent. Note: Chrome's speech recognition sends audio to Google.

    Where we fall short

    Webcam gaze is imprecise, no user trial with people with disabilities has been done, no screen-reader audit has been carried out, and the demo section on this page is a labelled simulation.

    Source: Bureau of Indian Standards. A free copy of IS 17802 (Part 1) is available from the BIS portal.
